In the harbor town of Braewick lived Kanar Quillick, married to Bressa Quillick, with their children. In order of birth these were the eldest a son, Codan Quillick; then a daughter, Kanelle Quillick; then a daughter, Ilra Quillick.

Voena Tallowenridge had married Codan Quillick some years before. The couple lived in Wilwick.

Wedan Tallowley and Felis Marrley were husband and wife. The couple lived in Harrowford.

Kanar Quillick worked as a tanner. Kanar Quillick was born at Yarlton.

Bressa Quillick worked as a weaver.

By trade Codan Quillick was a cooper. Folk called Codan Quillick "Tall Codan".

Kanelle Quillick worked as a mason. Folk called Kanelle Quillick "Red Kanelle".

By trade Ilra Quillick was a weaver. Ilra Quillick was born at Mireton.

By trade Breric Tallowenridge was a mason. Breric Tallowenridge had been born in Harrowmere.

Wera Tallowenridge was born at Mireton.

Volia Tallowenridge was born at Mireton.

Isnelle Thornwood was born at Ostcombe.

Nemor Marrley worked as a mason. Nemor Marrley was born at Elmford.

Among the possessions of Netho Ashson was the copper map.

Nemor Marrley kept the bone flute in a cedar box.

Among the possessions of Vowyn Thornwood was the iron ring.

The copper ledger belonged to Kanar Quillick, head of the Quillick household.

The garnet compass belonged to Serine Marrley.

The bone seal belonged to Hadan Holloway.

The iron seal belonged to Breric Tallowenridge.

Breric Tallowenridge kept the amber chalice in a cedar box.

The garnet ledger belonged to Isnelle Thornwood.

Tadrek Tallowley alone carried the knowledge that the old abbey at Harrowmere hides a second cellar.

Breric Tallowenridge alone carried the knowledge that the Thornwood mill ledger was rewritten after the flood.

Only Isssa Holloway knew that the Ashson family once owed a blood debt to the Quillson family.

Bressa Quillick alone carried the knowledge that a chest of silver lies buried beneath the well at Kelmere.

Only Illan Tallowley knew that the fire at the Torcombe granary was set on purpose.

Gamor Ashson alone carried the knowledge that the deed to the Fengate pastures was forged.

Webin Ashson alone carried the knowledge that the harbor master of Wilwick takes bribes.

Only Lurine Holloway knew that the bridge toll at Larkford is skimmed every month.

It was an old law of Elmford that the vault beneath its chapel would open only for a living person who held both the garnet compass and the brass mirror at once.

The toll bridge at Fengate belonged to the Tallowley family, and only those born a Tallowley, or presently married to one, were allowed to cross it.

By custom, the copper ledger always passed, on the death of whoever held it, to the eldest living child of that holder; no will could change this, and it passed without ceremony.



Chapter 2

It was the summer of the first year.

In the village of Wilwick. Breric meant to go to Bryncombe before the frost, but the roads flooded and the journey was put off.

In the river crossing at Mireton. Lurine could keep it no longer and told Thilan Holloway that the bridge toll at Larkford is skimmed every month.

In the river crossing at Yarlgate. The wife of Ilbin Thornworth won the silver lantern from Vodette Thornworth at cards, fairly, and kept it.

Ilbin packed a single trunk and moved from Yarlgate to Braewick.

Ornelle Thornworth went along to live in Braewick as well.

In the river crossing at Mireton. Thilan could keep it no longer and told Isssa that the bridge toll at Larkford is skimmed every month.

Hadan was apprenticed anew and became a shepherd.
