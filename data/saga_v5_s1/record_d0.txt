

Chapter 1

It was the spring of the first year.

The Corrmere household lived in the harbor town of Harrowford. Nymor Corrmere and his wife Ririne Corrmere had 4 children, all living at home: the eldest a son, Luric Corrmere; then a son, Motho Corrmere; then a daughter, Zadette Corrmere; then a son, Werald Corrmere.

The Haskson household lived in Yarlhaven. Welan Haskson and his wife Islde Haskson had 3 children, all living at home: the eldest a son, Thirald Haskson; then a son, Gaten Haskson; then a son, Nedan Haskson.

The Corrwood household lived in the hill farms of Ostgate. Thiric Corrwood and his wife Thidette Corrwood had 4 children, all living at home: the eldest a daughter, Ullia Corrwood; then a daughter, Pevie Corrwood; then a daughter, Darine Corrwood; then a son, Nyvus Corrwood.

In the hill farms of Ostgate lived Lurald Prydaby, married to Zaena Prydaby, with their children. In order of birth these were the eldest a son, Brebin Prydaby; then a son, Kanar Prydaby; then a daughter, Karine Prydaby; then a son, Haric Prydaby.

The Marroway household lived in the river crossing at Gulstead. Orric Marroway and his wife Vovie Marroway had 3 children, all living at home: the eldest a son, Nyvus Marroway; then a daughter, Romira Marroway; then a son, Elten Marroway.

In the hill farms of Kelwick lived Voten Quillett, married to Comira Quillett, with their children. In order of birth these were the eldest a son, Wedan Quillett; then a daughter, Aelia Quillett.

The Corrner household lived in the harbor town of Harrowford. Lulan Corrner and his wife Codette Corrner had 2 children, all living at home: the eldest a son, Thirald Corrner; then a daughter, Isena Corrner.

In the harbor town of Fencombe lived Uldan Grimworth, married to Thilia Grimworth, with their children. In order of birth these were the eldest a daughter, Ilssa Grimworth; then a daughter, Darine Grimworth; then a daughter, Yessa Grimworth; then a son, Nylan Grimworth.

The Stenner household lived in the village of Elmhaven. Garic Stenner and his wife Jomira Stenner had 3 children, all living at home: the eldest a daughter, Vowyn Stenner; then a daughter, Rolis Stenner; then a son, Ismor Stenner.

In the market town of Brynby lived Brerald Marrworth, married to Nemira Marrworth, with their children. In order of birth these were the eldest a son, Jomor Marrworth; then a son, Sebin Marrworth.

Haric Prydaby had married Romira Marroway some years before. The couple lived in Ostgate.

Thirald Corrner had married Ilssa Grimworth some years before. The couple lived in Harrowford.

Vowyn Stenner and Nedan Haskson were husband and wife. The couple lived in Elmhaven.

Darine Corrwood and Nyvus Marroway were husband and wife. The couple lived in Ostgate.

Kanar Prydaby and Aelia Quillett were husband and wife. The couple lived in Ostgate.

Isena Corrner had married Nyvus Corrwood some years before. The couple lived in Harrowford.

Nymor Corrmere worked as a herbalist.

By trade Ririne Corrmere was a scribe.

Luric Corrmere was born at Yarlhaven.

Motho Corrmere had been born in Gulstead.

Zadette Corrmere was born at Torcombe.

By trade Islde Haskson was a chandler.

Thirald Haskson was born at Fencombe.

By trade Nedan Haskson was a fisher. Nedan Haskson was born at Kelwick. Folk called Nedan Haskson "Red Nedan".

Thidette Corrwood worked as a miller.

By trade Ullia Corrwood was a chandler. Ullia Corrwood had been born in Elmhaven.

By trade Darine Corrwood was a scribe. Folk called Darine Corrwood "Tall Darine".

Nyvus Corrwood had been born in Torcombe.

By trade Zaena Prydaby was a scribe. Folk called Zaena Prydaby "Old Zaena".

By trade Brebin Prydaby was a cooper. Brebin Prydaby had been born in Wendhaven. Folk called Brebin Prydaby "Red Brebin".

Kanar Prydaby was born at Gulstead.

By trade Karine Prydaby was a mason. Karine Prydaby was born at Elmhaven. Folk called Karine Prydaby "Karine the Younger".

By trade Haric Prydaby was a miller. Haric Prydaby had been born in Salby.

Garic Stenner kept the amber ring in a cedar box.

The garnet flute belonged to Werald Corrmere.

The silver map belonged to Vowyn Stenner.

Among the possessions of Brebin Prydaby was the jade chalice.

Nymor Corrmere kept the copper map in a cedar box.

Romira Marroway kept the amber flute in a cedar box.

Among the possessions of Thirald Haskson was the ivory locket.

Among the possessions of Garic Stenner was the garnet comb.

The amber compass belonged to Brebin Prydaby.

Among the possessions of Nyvus Marroway was the jade ring.

Only Werald Corrmere knew that the harbor master of Coldwick takes bribes.

Nyvus Marroway alone carried the knowledge that the Stenner mill ledger was rewritten after the flood.

Only Thidette Corrwood knew that the fire at the Yarlhaven granary was set on purpose.

Only Darine Grimworth knew that a chest of silver lies buried beneath the well at Coldwick.

Only Pevie Corrwood knew that the Marroway family once owed a blood debt to the Haskson family.

Only Thirald Haskson knew that the bridge toll at Kelwick is skimmed every month.

Lulan Corrner alone carried the knowledge that the old abbey at Ostton hides a second cellar.

Only Werald Corrmere knew that the deed to the Gulstead pastures was forged.

It was an old law of Coldstead that the vault beneath its chapel would open only for a living person who held both the silver map and the copper map at once.

The toll bridge at Ostton belonged to the Stenner family, and only those born a Stenner, or presently married to one, were allowed to cross it.

By custom, the jade dagger always passed, on the death of whoever held it, to the eldest living child of that holder; no will could change this, and it passed without ceremony.



Chapter 2

It was the summer of the first year.

In the harbor town of Harrowford. Before the autumn was out, Motho Corrmere took Yessa Grimworth in marriage at Harrowford. The couple lived in Harrowford from then on.

Isena Corrner gave birth to a son, named Romor Corrwood; the father was Nyvus Corrwood. The family lived in Harrowford.

Lulan Corrner could keep it no longer and told the wife of Nymor Corrmere that the old abbey at Ostton hides a second cellar.

In the hill farms of Kelwick. Comira Quillett meant to go to Coldby before the frost, but the roads flooded and the journey was put off.

In the hill farms of Ostgate. Red Brebin gave the iron dagger to Nyvus Marroway.

In the river crossing at Gulstead. Elten Marroway and Darine Grimworth were wed at Gulstead, with half the parish looking on. The couple lived in Gulstead from then on.



Chapter 3

It was the autumn of the first year.

In the market town of Brynby. Sebin gave Brerald Marrworth a solemn promise to carry a sealed letter to Torcombe.

In the hill farms of Ostgate. Before the winter was out, Pevie Corrwood took Jomor in marriage at Ostgate. The couple lived in Ostgate from then on.

In the harbor town of Harrowford. Werald could keep it no longer and told Ilssa Grimworth that the deed to the Gulstead pastures was forged.

Werald Corrmere gave the garnet flute to Ririne Corrmere.

In the river crossing at Gulstead. In a letter that the husband of Darine Grimworth burned after reading, the second daughter of Thilia Grimworth revealed that a chest of silver lies buried beneath the well at Coldwick.

The wife of Elten Marroway packed a single trunk and moved from Gulstead to Harrowford.

Elten went along to live in Harrowford as well.

In the village of Elmhaven. Garic Stenner gave the garnet comb to Ismor.

In the hill farms of Ostgate. Brebin Prydaby pressed the jade chalice into the hands of Jomor Marrworth and would take nothing for it.

In the hill farms of Kelwick. In the chapel at Kelwick, the only son of Comira Quillett married Ullia Corrwood. The couple lived in Kelwick from then on.

Jomor Marrworth swore to Thiric Corrwood to plant an orchard on the slope above Coldstead, and shook on it.

In the hill farms of Kelwick. A rumor started by Comira Quillett had it that Brebin Prydaby was dead of plague; Comira Quillett had it from a peddler.

Comira found work in Coldwick, moved there, and did not return to Kelwick.

It was the summer of the second year.

In the hill farms of Ostgate. In a letter that Thidette Corrwood burned after reading, Pevie Corrwood revealed that the Marroway family once owed a blood debt to the Haskson family.

Jomor Marrworth went along to live in Harrowford as well.

The wife of Thiric Corrwood rode out of Ostgate; by the next market day she was living in Yarlhaven.

Thiric Corrwood followed her to Yarlhaven within the month.

In the hill farms of Coldstead. A parcel from Ismor reached Thiric Corrwood; inside was the garnet comb, now his to keep.

In the harbor town of Harrowford. True to his word to Thiric Corrwood, Jomor Marrworth finished what he had promised (plant an orchard on the slope above Coldstead).

Late one night Lulan confided to Nymor Corrmere that the harbor master of Coldwick takes bribes.

Ririne Corrmere swore to Codette Corrner to rebuild the fallen wall at Yarlhaven, and shook on it.

In the harbor town of Harrowford. Isena Corrner gave Rolis a solemn promise to teach the children of Wendhaven their letters.
